[s5-p000] Expenditure cascade and
[s5-p001] upscale emulation
[s5-p002] High-status consumption items “trickle down” to lower
[s5-p003] classes in an “expenditure cascade”: expectations and    expenditures by lower classes expand.
[s5-p004] Schorr: upscale emulation is an attempt to emulate lives of elites, primarily celebrities.
[s5-p005] • “New consumerism”: not keeping up with the Joneses,  but with the Jolie-Pitts.
[s5-p006] Aspirational gap: consumption desires consistently outrun incomes.
[s5-p007] Once an item is widely popular, its value for conspicuous   consumption is lost.

[s6-p000] Bourdieu: class and “good taste”
[s6-p001] High-status consumption and wealth are related, but their  relationship is culturally complex.
[s6-p002] Consumption habits (tastes) both reflect and reinforce     inequalities.
[s6-p003] •    It is not enough to be able to afford a consumption item. One must know which item to choose and how to consume it.
[s6-p004] Social inequality is naturalized as individual good taste.
[s6-p005] •  Is there a brand of coffee you avoid? Why?
[s6-p006] •  Do all your friends agree on which brands to drink and which to     avoid?

[s7-p000] Three forms of capital
[s7-p001] Economic: money.
[s7-p002] Social: access to resources (support, information, opportunities) through an individual’s social network.
[s7-p003] • “It’s not what you know, but whom you know.”
[s7-p004] Cultural: knowledge, skills and preference highly valued within a particular culture.
[s7-p005] •  Your professor has invited you to a party at the Department of
[s7-p006] Sociology. What will you talk about with people you meet there?
[s7-p007] These forms can be exchanged for one another.
[s7-p008] One may be high on one form of capital, but lacking in another.
[s7-p009] •  Name a social group high on economic capital, but low on cultural one. How about a group high on cultural capital, but low on economic one?
[s7-p010] © 2017 Taylor & Francis

[s11-p000] Coffee in a global commodity chain
[s11-p001] Commodity chain: the path a commodity takes from its       conception and design, through manufacturing, retailing     and consumption, to its end as waste.
[s11-p002] The “coffee paradox”:
[s11-p003] 1.   “Coffee crisis” in the Global South: coffee growers are poorly paid and have no control over the coffee
[s11-p004] commodity chain; over-supply on the global market; and
[s11-p005] 2.   “Coffee boom” in the Global North: distributors add
[s11-p006] value to the product by emphasizing its symbolic value and retain most of the profit in the commodity chain.

[s15-p000] Research tool: survey research
[s15-p001] Administering a standardized questionnaire to a sample  of respondents, and analyzing their responses.
[s15-p002] Sample: a small subset of respondents, representative of a larger population research is interested in.
[s15-p003] •  You are interested in coffee-drinking habits of students at your
[s15-p004] university/college. Do you need to get each and every one of them  to fill out a survey? How will you choose your respondents?
[s15-p005] A quantitative method: data are numerical.
[s15-p006] Goal: compare characteristics, opinions and actions           across individuals and groups in the population.
[s15-p007] •  You want to find out if there are differences in coffee-drinking habits of men and women. Which questions do you need to ask in order to find this out?
